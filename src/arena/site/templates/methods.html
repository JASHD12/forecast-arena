Scoring

Brier and log scores on probabilities clipped to [0.01, 0.99]; skill against the base rate on shared questions; calibration diagrams with expected calibration error and the Murphy decomposition; 95% day-block bootstrap intervals for every score and paired difference. A difference is called supported only when its interval excludes zero.

A worked example

Three questions, two forecasters (made-up numbers, real arithmetic). Outcome 1 = YES, 0 = NO; Brier = (p − outcome)², averaged.

QuestionOutcomeBase rate pBrierModel pBrier
A10.500.25000.800.0400
B00.600.36000.300.0900
C00.400.16000.995 → clipped 0.990.9801
Mean0.25670.3700

The model was better on A and B, but one overconfident miss (C) makes its average worse than the base rate: skill = 1 − 0.3700 / 0.2567 = −0.44. Clipping to 0.99 keeps a single answer from being infinitely bad on the log score, but confident mistakes still cost a lot. With three questions no interval could exclude zero, so the site would say "too early to tell".
